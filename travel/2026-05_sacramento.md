## 📍 The Places — Detailed Guide

### 1. Fairytale Town
**3901 Land Park Dr, Sacramento · William Land Park**
**Hours:** 9:00 AM – 4:00 PM (guests in by 4 PM may stay until 5 PM)
**Admission:** $10/person · Free under age 2

Fairytale Town is one of Sacramento's most beloved local treasures — a whimsical, storybook-themed amusement park nestled inside William Land Park that has been delighting kids since 1959. Unlike loud, overstimulating theme parks, Fairytale Town is calm, shaded, and genuinely magical for young children.

The park is organized around classic fairy tale and nursery rhyme settings. Kids can climb into the Old Woman's Shoe, slide down the beanstalk, explore the crooked house, and wander through sets inspired by Jack and Jill, Humpty Dumpty, and more. There's also a small petting zoo with farm animals, a stage for puppet shows and live performances, and a handful of gentle rides sized just right for the under-8 crowd. The scale of everything is perfectly designed for little legs and big imaginations.

Budget about 2.5–3 hours here.

**Pro tip:** Check [fairytaletown.org](https://www.fairytaletown.org/plan-your-visit/) for advance tickets. Holiday weekends can sell out. Bring snacks — there are picnic areas throughout.

### 2. Old Sacramento State Historic Park
**Old Sacramento Waterfront, Sacramento**
**Hours:** Open daily · Most streets accessible anytime · Shops/restaurants vary
**Admission:** Free to explore
**Best for:** Everyone — walkable, photogenic, and historically rich

Old Sacramento is the beating heart of California's Gold Rush history, and it's one of the most atmospheric neighborhoods in the state. Twenty-eight acres of carefully preserved 19th-century commercial buildings line cobblestone streets along the east bank of the Sacramento River, giving the whole district the feel of stepping back in time.

What makes it special isn't just the history — it's how alive the neighborhood feels. Wooden boardwalks run along the storefronts. The Tower Bridge (painted a gorgeous gold) frames views across the river. Horse-drawn carriages occasionally clip-clop through. Shops sell old-fashioned candy, gold panning kits, and handmade goods. For a 6-year-old, the tactile experience of cobblestones, riverboats, and the sights and sounds of the waterfront is endlessly entertaining.

The California State Railroad Museum sits right in the heart of Old Sacramento (see below), and the Delta King — a historic paddlewheel riverboat permanently docked on the river — is worth a look even just from the outside.

**Why it works for your trip:** It's free, endlessly photogenic, and a natural Saturday morning stop before the Railroad Museum opens. After lunch at Joe's Crab Shack, the waterfront stroll gives you a relaxed way to spend the early afternoon before checking in. Give it about an hour; more if the kiddo wants to browse shops.

**Pro tip:** Try the candy stores along Front Street — they're a hit with kids. And grab a photo on the wooden boardwalk with the Tower Bridge in the background.

### 3. California State Capitol & Capitol Park
**1315 10th St, Sacramento**
**Hours:** Grounds open daily · Building tours Mon–Fri (limited weekend access)
**Admission:** Free
**Best for:** A scenic, easy late-afternoon stop — especially the park

The California State Capitol is one of the most beautiful government buildings in the United States, a grand Neoclassical dome rising above downtown Sacramento surrounded by 40 acres of meticulously kept park. Even if you don't go inside (tours are limited on weekends), Capitol Park alone is worth the stop.

The park is a genuine horticultural showcase — the grounds contain trees and plants from every state in the nation plus dozens of countries, collected over more than a century. The California Veterans Memorial sits on the grounds, along with rose gardens, camellia groves, and wide shaded lawns. In late May, everything is in full bloom and lush green before the summer heat fully arrives.

For your 6-year-old, the park is essentially a massive, gorgeous playground for running around — open space, interesting statues to investigate, squirrels everywhere. It's a lovely way to decompress after a morning of structured sightseeing before dinner.

**Why it works for your trip:** It's a 10-minute drive from Old Sacramento and a natural next stop on Saturday afternoon. No tickets, no schedules — just show up and enjoy. About 45–60 minutes is plenty.

**Pro tip:** The building's west steps (facing 10th Street) give one of the best photos in Sacramento — the dome framed by palm trees. Late afternoon light on a May evening is beautiful here.

### 4. California State Railroad Museum
**125 I St, Old Sacramento**
**Hours:** 10:00 AM – 5:00 PM daily (tickets sold until 4:30 PM)
**Admission:** Adults $12 · Ages 6–17: $6 · Under 6: Free
**Best for:** Train-obsessed kids (and adults)

The California State Railroad Museum is widely considered one of the finest railroad museums in the world, and it earns that reputation. The vast main hall houses more than 20 meticulously restored locomotives and rail cars from the 19th and early 20th centuries — massive, gleaming, awe-inspiring machines that you can walk right up to and in many cases climb inside.

For a 6-year-old, standing next to a full-size steam locomotive is a jaw-dropping experience. The scale is simply impossible to comprehend until you're standing there. The museum does a wonderful job of making the history accessible and exciting — interactive exhibits, period dining car displays, a recreated 1860s station platform, and the story of how the Transcontinental Railroad was built right here in Sacramento. There are sleeping car berths you can peek into, a mail sorting car, and an enormous model railroad layout that runs throughout the day.

From April through September, the museum also operates **excursion train rides** departing from the Central Pacific Passenger Station (Front & K Streets, just steps away) — a 40-minute round trip along the Sacramento River on a real vintage train. Check in advance for Memorial Day weekend availability; these sell out.

**Why it works for your trip:** Saturday morning gives you fresh energy after the drive up. The museum opens right at 10 AM, the excursion train departs at 11:15, and Joe's Crab Shack is a 0.3 mi walk from the station. The whole morning flows as one continuous loop.

**Pro tip:** Arrive right at 10:00 AM to beat the crowds and get the best experience with the locomotives before the museum fills up. Ask a docent about the excursion train schedule when you arrive.

### 5. Sacramento Children's Museum
**2701 Prospect Park Dr, Rancho Cordova** *(same area as Best Western Plus — no extra driving)*
**Hours:** Sat–Sun 10:00 AM – 4:00 PM
**Admission:** $10/person · Under 1 free
**Best for:** Ages birth–8 — perfect for your 6-year-old
**Time needed:** 2–3 hours

20 interactive exhibits designed for hands-on, play-based learning. Highlights include a wind tunnel (Airways), an art studio with folk art from around the world, a pretend-play neighborhood, an Imagineering Zone with engineering challenges, water play, and STEM pop-up programs. Clean, well-staffed, and consistently well-reviewed.

Since it's in Rancho Cordova near the hotel, it's a low-effort option — **use as a backup** if Fairytale Town is too crowded or the weather is hot. Not in the main itinerary.

### 6. Wolfe Heights Estates Winery
**9440 Bar Du Ln, Vineyard/Sacramento, CA 95829**
**Wedding:** Sunday, May 24 · 5:00 PM – 11:00 PM

Wolfe Heights is an 82-acre private estate vineyard tucked southeast of Sacramento — the kind of place that makes guests feel like they've been transported to Napa Valley without the two-hour drive. The property grows Tempranillo, Cabernet Sauvignon, and Petite Sirah, and the setting is breathtakingly beautiful: rolling vineyard rows, warm golden light in the evening hours, and an intimate, rustic-elegant atmosphere perfect for a wedding.

For your family, the vineyard setting gives your 6-year-old room to roam during the cocktail hour and early reception. The property is spacious and outdoorsy, which tends to work in favor of young kids at evening events. As the night winds down toward 11 PM, the drive back to your Best Western Plus hotel is mercifully short — about 10–15 minutes south on Elk Grove Florin Road.

**Navigation note:** The address takes you to a rural road. Download the route offline before you go — cell service can be inconsistent in this area. Leave by 4:15 PM to arrive relaxed and early. And remember: wear shoes that can handle grass and gravel paths gracefully.
