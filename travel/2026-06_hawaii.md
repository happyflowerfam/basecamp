🌌 **Optional evening — Mauna Kea stargazing (Visitor Station, 9,200 ft)**
A dormant volcano + one of the best dark-sky spots on Earth. Good fit for the relaxed Hilo day.
- **No 4WD needed:** drive to the **Visitor Information Station (VIS) at 9,200 ft** — paved road, ~45–60 min from Hilo via Saddle Road. The 2WD rental is fine. The *summit* (4WD-only) is off-limits to under-16s anyway, so the VIS is our version regardless.
- **Go for sunset + stars.** Plan an early Hilo dinner first, or pack dinner.
- ⚠️ **Cold** — 30s–40s°F at night even in June. Bring the summit warm layers + rain jacket (already packing them). Beanies/gloves if we have them.
- ⚠️ **Altitude:** 9,200 ft straight from sea level — acclimatize ~30 min, hydrate, watch Samā for headache/nausea. Skip if she's wiped.
- ⚠️ ~1 hr drive back in the dark on Saddle Road — go in well-rested, fuel up before.
- Check before going: confirm the **VIS hours + whether the nightly stargazing program is running** (it's been intermittently suspended) — [Maunakea VIS](https://www.maunakea.com/visitor-information-station/) · clear-sky forecast.
- **Easy to skip** if Day 6 already feels full or the eruption glow (Day 7) is the priority.

🎒 Pack breakfast & lunch — GF options scarce inside the park · layers + rain jacket for summit (12–15°F cooler than coast) · lightweight + sun protection for Chain of Craters Road

Check before you go: [Kīlauea webcam](https://www.youtube.com/live/gXKuUyKt8mc?si=fPu-FoQEeF9MerHX) · [NP weather](https://www.nps.gov/havo/planyourvisit/weather.htm) · [USGS HVO daily updates](https://www.usgs.gov/volcanoes/kilauea/volcano-updates) · Day 8 is available for a second Volcanoes day if you want more

🌋 **Episode 49 watch:** Kīlauea erupts in episodes (hours of lava fountaining, then days paused). Episode 48 ended Jun 1; USGS estimates the next episode ~Jun 11–16 — **right on our park days.** If an episode lands while we're here, that's the spectacular version.
- **Check [USGS HVO](https://www.usgs.gov/volcanoes/kilauea/volcano-updates) ~Jun 8–10** for the Episode 49 forecast
- If it's forecast to hit Jun 11 or 12, prioritize being at the park — **after dark for the caldera glow** (Uēkahuna / Kīlauea Overlook)
- Viewing is from overlooks only (crater is a closed area); an active episode can change park access + air quality, so check alerts day-of

**🏛️ Welcome Center — Kīlauea Military Camp (KMC)**
Open daily **9:00am–4:45pm** · Kīlauea Visitor Center closed for renovation until at least summer 2026 — KMC is the temporary hub
- Rangers, Junior Ranger books, passport stamps, park store all here
- ⚠️ Park in the **ballfield lot next to KMC** — not in front of the camp
- First stop of the day: pick up Junior Ranger booklet + check conditions

**🎙️ Life on the Edge — Ranger Program**
Daily at **10:00am · 11:00am · 1:00pm · 2:00pm** · ~20 min · Free
Meet the ranger at **Uēkahuna parking area**
Blends geology with Native Hawaiian moʻolelo (stories), chants, and historical accounts — how eruptions have shaped both the landscape and Hawaiian history.
**🎙️ Explore the Summit — Ranger Program**
Daily at **10:30am · 12:30pm** · 30–45 min · Free
Meet at the **U.S. Flagpole at Kīlauea Military Camp** (same location as Welcome Center)
Covers geology, Native Hawaiian history, and unique native plants and animals.

## 🎒 Logistics & Tips

- **Sun protection**: Hawaii sun is intense; reef-safe sunscreen required at many beaches
- **Rain on north shore Kauai**: It rains — bring a light layer and embrace it; everything is green because of it
- **Rip currents**: Real hazard on Hawaii beaches. North shore Kauai (Hanalei, Ke'e) and Punalu'u black sand beach (Big Island) can be dangerous — look-don't-swim unless conditions are clearly calm. **Lydgate Beach Park** (Kauai) has enclosed man-made snorkel pools — safest option for Samā. Always check conditions before entering open water.
- **Packed lunches**: Build a simple rotation — GF crackers + cheese + salami + fruit + a sweet.
- **Gluten tips**: Call restaurants ahead; Hawaiian food is naturally GF-friendly
