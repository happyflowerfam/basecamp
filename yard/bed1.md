# Bed 1 — Adjacent to house

## Conditions
- **Location:** Against house wall, Foster City
- **Sun:** Full sun all day
- **Water:** Irrigated
- **Microclimate:** Wind protection from house; some reflected heat from wall
- **Soil:** Heavy clay (bay fill) — same as rest of yard

## Plants

| Plant              | Type            | Water needs | Notes                                                                                                                                                 |
| ------------------ | --------------- | ----------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Echeveria** (×2) | Succulent       | Low         | Overwatering risk in irrigated bed — clay holds moisture; ensure good drainage around crown                                                           |
| **Aeonium**        | Succulent       | Low         | Well-suited to Foster City's cool coastal climate; goes summer dormant in heat but mild summers here mean it stays active longer                      |
| **Ripple jade**    | Succulent       | Low         | Same overwatering concern as echeveria; clay soil + regular irrigation is the main risk                                                               |
| **Hibiscus**       | Flowering shrub | Moderate    | Needs regular water and full sun — well placed. Watch for competition from Norway maple roots                                                         |
| **Norway maple**   | Deciduous tree  | Moderate    | **Problem plant.** Stem removed but roots too deep to dig out — roots will compete with hibiscus and risk house foundation. Treat stump with drill-and-fill (concentrated glyphosate) to stop resprouting, then hire stump grinder to remove |
| **Dragon fruit**   | Tropical cactus | Low–Moderate | **Struggling** — Foster City's cool summers are the main problem; dragon fruit needs heat to thrive and fruit. Clay soil and overwatering compound the issue. Best chance: amend soil around base with coarse grit for drainage; house wall reflected heat is its best asset here |
| **Pomegranate**    | Fruiting shrub/tree | Low–Moderate | Small shrub — **remove completely.** Do not just cut stems; will resprout from root crown. Dig out whole root ball with trowel while spring soil is moist — comes out easily at this size |
| **Curry leaves**   | Herb / small tree | Moderate  | Frost-sensitive but surviving in ground — wall protection helps. Prefers well-drained soil; amend around base if clay is compacting                   |
| **Lemon balm**     | Herb            | Moderate    | Spreads aggressively — cut back hard regularly to keep contained; will take over the bed if left unchecked                                            |

## Key issues

- **Norway maple:** stem removed; root ~2cm diameter, at least 6" deep — too deep to dig out cleanly. Two removal options:
  - **Starve it (preferred):** cut every new sprout immediately as it appears all season; root system will exhaust itself within one season
  - **Chemical:** paint Garlon 4 (triclopyr) directly onto fresh drill holes in stump — do not spray; breaks down faster than Tordon and safer near hibiscus and house foundation
  - Avoid Tordon RTU (picloram) — too soil-persistent near house and other plants
- **Dragon fruit:** Foster City is marginal climate for dragon fruit — cool summers mean it will struggle to fruit and may barely survive. The house wall is its best microspot for reflected heat. Improve drainage around the base with coarse grit amendment; reduce irrigation to this plant specifically.
- **Pomegranate:** too close to house — move now while still small. Needs a warm spot with space; roots will otherwise reach irrigation lines and foundation.
- **Mixed water needs:** succulents (low water) are in the same irrigated bed as hibiscus, curry leaves, and lemon balm (moderate water). Succulents in clay soil with regular irrigation are at risk of root rot. Consider moving succulents to a drier spot or raised area with amended soil.
- **Lemon balm:** will spread into the lawn and surrounding areas if not cut back to the crown 2–3 times per season.
