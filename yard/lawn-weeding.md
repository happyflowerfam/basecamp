# Spring weeding — Foster City lawn

Part of the lawn plan — see [lawn.md](lawn.md) for seasonal maintenance and [yard-design.md](yard-design.md) for site conditions.

Work when soil is moist for easier root removal. Priority is removing anything before it sets seed.

### Keep

| Plant            | Notes                                                                                                     |
| ---------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **White clover** | Nitrogen fixer, drought tolerant, stays green in summer — a lawn asset                                   |
| **Self-heal**    | Low creeping perennial; tolerates 3" mowing, thrives in clay, pollinator-friendly — fits the eco approach |

### Tolerate / Manage

| Plant                   | Action                        | Tools           | Notes                                                                                                                                                                                                                                          |
| ----------------------- | ----------------------------- | --------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Mouse-ear chickweed** | Tolerate                      | —               | Stays low; mowing controls it well; Foster City's cool moist climate favors it but it won't dominate a dense lawn                                                                                                                              |
| **Wild strawberry**     | Thin near play area           | Herb snips      | Snip runners at the node; runners create uneven surface underfoot — remove from center, tolerate at edges                                                                                                                                      |
| **Wood sorrel**         | Mow down; remove if spreading | Hand cultivator | Upright type is controlled by regular mowing; if it's creeping Oxalis (*O. corniculata*, tiny yellow flowers, heart-shaped leaves) remove aggressively — it thrives in Foster City's moist clay and is very hard to eradicate once established |

### Remove

| Plant                 | Action                  | Tools           | Notes                                                                                                                                                                          |
| --------------------- | ----------------------- | --------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Hairy bittercress** | Remove urgently         | Hands           | Seed pods explode and scatter when touched — pull whole rosette now, bag immediately, do not compost. Shallow taproot releases easily in moist spring soil                     |
| **Annual bluegrass**  | Remove before seed set  | Hand cultivator | Pale blue-green clumpy grass (*Poa annua*); loosen clump base with cultivator and lift out. Dies back in summer leaving bare patches, so removing now also prevents gaps later |
| **Annual knawel**     | Pull before seeding     | Hands           | Wiry mat-forming annual; shallow roots, easy to pull in spring                                                                                                                 |
| **Petty spurge**      | Remove before seed set  | Hands (gloves)  | Milky white sap — wear gloves, irritates skin and eyes. Hand-pull while soil is moist; taproot comes out cleanly                                                               |
| **Hairy pennywort** (dollarweed) | Remove in fall; reduce moisture now | Trowel (fall)   | Creates soft spongy mats — tripping hazard for kids. Large patches signal a drainage or over-irrigation problem: check for pooling water or a nearby head running too long. Don't dig out a large patch now — fragments regrow. Reduce irrigation in that zone, mow to 3", then aerate heavily and dig out properly in fall before overseeding with RTF |
| **French sorrel**     | Dig out                 | Trowel          | Coarse and upright; dig out the root crown with trowel to prevent regrowth                                                                                                     |
| **Houndstongue**      | Remove before flowering | Ergo weeder     | Pull the rosette in spring before it bolts; burr seeds cling to clothing and spread widely                                                                                     |
| **Wild lettuce**      | Remove promptly         | Ergo weeder     | Gets 3–6 ft tall and smothers surrounding plants; use ergo weeder to get under the taproot while still a rosette                                                               |
| **Scarlet pimpernel** | Remove before seed set  | Hands           | Low-creeping annual with small orange-red flowers and oval opposite leaves. Mildly toxic if ingested, can irritate skin — priority removal on a kids' lawn. Shallow roots, easy to hand-pull in moist soil |

## One-season weedicide option (pennywort and wild lettuce)

For large patches that are impractical to hand-remove, a targeted iron-based herbicide is the least harmful option and a pragmatic one-season bridge before the fall fix.

**Product:** Fiesta (or equivalent iron-based herbicide)
- Active ingredient: ferric sodium EDTA (iron)
- Kills broadleaf weeds; does not harm grass
- Approved for organic use; no soil residue
- Safe around kids and pets once dry
- Available at OSH, Ace Hardware, or online

**How to use:**
- Spot-spray the pennywort patch and any wild lettuce rosettes — do not broadcast the whole lawn
- Apply on a dry day with no rain forecast for 24 hours
- Expect browning within 1–2 days
- A second application 2–3 weeks later may be needed for pennywort

**This is a reset, not a cure.** Pennywort will return if the underlying drainage issue isn't fixed. Follow up in fall: aerate heavily, dig out the dead patch, topdress with compost, and overseed with RTF to fill the gap and suppress regrowth long-term.

## Mower recommendation

**Get your own mower** — a gardener will typically mow at 2–2.5", too short for RTF and bad for weed suppression. Owning the mower means control over height and timing, both of which matter for this lawn.

**What to get:** battery-powered push mower with mulch and bag options
- **Ego** or **Greenworks** — both work well for 2,000 sq ft; Ego has better battery life
- Must have a bag attachment and be settable to 3"
- Always mow at 3" — right for RTF, play use, and weed suppression

**On clippings:**
- Normal practice: leave clippings on the lawn (free nitrogen, good for clay soil)
- **Right now while Poa annua is actively seeding:** use the bag to collect clippings and remove seed heads before they drop. Switch back to mulching once the flush is over.
