# Action Plan — Foster City Yard

## Immediate (April — now)

- [ ] **Bait for pavement ants in driveway** — use a slow-acting bait (e.g., Terro liquid ant bait stations) placed at ant trails near driveway cracks; workers carry it back to the colony; do not spray — kills foragers but not the nest
- [ ] **Complete yard-design.md** — fill in TBD fields: shade, irrigation, plants, lengths/areas for hedge, flower bed, driveway bed, Bed 3 location, front lawn shade
- [ ] **Complete toolkit.md** — add any tools owned beyond the six already listed (ergo weeder, folding saw, herb snips, trowel, hand cultivator, bypass pruner)
- [x] **Buy mower** — battery-powered push mower with mulch and bag options (Ego or Greenworks); must set to 3"
- [x] **Mow lawn at 3"** — do this week; knocks down weed seed heads across the whole lawn in one pass
- [ ] **Pull hairy bittercress** — urgent; seed pods explode when touched; bag immediately, do not compost
- [ ] **Spot spray Fiesta** on pennywort patch and wild lettuce rosettes — iron-based herbicide (ferric sodium EDTA); approved for organic use, no soil residue, safe around kids and pets once dry. Available at OSH, Ace Hardware, or online. Apply on a dry day, no rain forecast for 24 hours; second application may be needed 2–3 weeks later for pennywort
- [ ] **Cut and paint stumps** — cut Norway maple, pomegranate, and any avocado sprouts as low as possible; immediately paint cut surface with **Garlon 4 (triclopyr)**. Repeat on any new sprouts. Do not use glyphosate — triclopyr is more effective on woody resprouters

## Spring (April–May)

- [ ] **Fertilize fruit trees** — apply now (late but still effective); use light dose (~75% of package recommendation) and water in well
  - Apple, plum, cherry plum, pomegranate: balanced 10-10-10 slow-release granular or fruit tree formula
  - Lemon: citrus-specific fertilizer (Espoma Citrus-tone or Dr. Earth Fruit Tree); lemon can also get a second application in August
  - Skip June application for deciduous trees since fertilizing late; set reminder for late February next year

- [ ] **Install Rachio 3** — ordered May 2, 2026. Replaces existing controller box, same wiring. DIY install ~30 min or hire irrigation contractor for under an hour. Set up weather-based ET scheduling and cycle-and-soak for clay soil once installed.

- [ ] **Snip wild lettuce** at base with herb snips if already tall — prevents seeding; don't bother pulling the taproot
- [ ] **Pull stray potatoes** in Bed 2 — easy in moist spring soil
- [ ] **Cut Norway maple and pomegranate sprouts** as they appear — paint each with Garlon 4 immediately

- [ ] **Remove tree support stakes** — untie or cut ties and pull stakes out; wiggle if base is rotted into soil
  - **Treated wood** (greenish tint, small injection slits, chemical smell): take to San Mateo County drop-off, 30 Disposal Road, San Carlos — free for residents
  - **Untreated wood** (plain pale/grey timber): cut to under 4 ft, put in green waste bin
  - **If unsure:** treat as treated and take to San Carlos
  - **If in good condition:** post on Nextdoor or Facebook Marketplace free section — will go quickly

## Summer (June–August)

- [ ] **Reduce irrigation** in pennywort area — find and adjust the zone or head overwatering that patch
- [ ] **Hand water** lavender and rosemary if planted in spring — weekly until established
- [ ] **Monitor hibiscus** in Bed 2 for summer stress — hand water if needed or move to Bed 1

## Fall (September–October) — key action season

- [ ] **Aerate lawn** — mandatory given clay soil and foot traffic; extra passes on pennywort patch
- [ ] **Dig out pennywort patch** — after aerating, roots easier to follow; topdress with compost
- [ ] **Overseed lawn** with RTF + microclover (~7 lbs per 1,000 sq ft) — target first weekend of October
- [ ] **Topdress with compost** (1/4") and work into aeration holes
- [ ] **Plant Bed 2 additions** — lavender, rosemary, salvia leucantha, yarrow
- [ ] **Prune cherry plums** — August–September for size control; budget ~$2,000 for all 10 trees (can split over two years)
- [ ] **Apply gypsum** — 25 lbs per 1,000 sq ft across lawn to loosen clay structure

## Ongoing — every 3 months (January, April, July, October)

- [ ] **Check Spectracide Terminate termite stakes** — walk the perimeter and inspect each stake:
  - The pop-up indicator on top should be flush with the cap — no activity
  - If the indicator has popped up, termites have been detected — remove stake and inspect cartridge for live termites, mud tubes, or damaged wood
  - If termites found, treat immediately (Spectracide sells a treatment product) and contact a pest control professional
  - Keep stakes clear of lawn overgrowth so indicator is visible
  - Replace cartridge annually even if not triggered
  - **Most important check: spring (April)** — peak termite swarming season in Northern California
